Which brief should Freshroots build first?

Deepa's 90-day mandate · MESA AI-Native Track, Term 2, Case 01 · Assessed with the Nine Checks and the Five Pre-Build Decisions · 22 Oct 2026
Build Demand Planning first. It is the only brief that passes all nine checks. Distributor Service fails a kill switch: there is no input data. Operations Intelligence fails sizing and ownership checks: the bottleneck is who acts on the data, not the report. Of the three briefs, Demand Planning has the largest quantified loss (₹28–34 lakh a quarter), data that already exists, and rules the planner has already written down. Those rules cover 80–85% of decisions.

2 · Five Pre-Build Decisions for Demand Planning

1 · Outcome definition: what must measurably change

  • Fewer stockouts and fewer write-offs of perishable stock, measured in rupees each quarter against the ₹28–34 L baseline.
  • More SKUs sitting inside their target stock range. That range is 6–14 weeks of stock for low-perishability SKUs and 3–4 weeks for high-perishability ones.
  • The planning cycle moves from monthly with data 3–4 weeks old to twice a week with data under 1 week old.

2 · Baseline: the current state in numbers

From the brief
  • Stockout losses: ₹18–22 L a quarter
  • Overstock write-offs: ₹10–12 L a quarter, mostly perishables
  • Cycle: once a month, 2 planner days, data 3–4 weeks old when used
  • Rules cover 80–85% of decisions; 15–20% are judgment calls
From the SAP export (snapshot 19 Oct, week 42)
  • 15 SKUs evaluated, 0 data-quality errors
  • 8 of 15 (53%) within their target range
  • 5 (33%) at stockout risk, including both perishable lines with under 2 weeks of stock
  • 2 (13%) overstocked: Quinoa at 22 weeks, Coconut Water at 4.1 weeks against a 4-week maximum

3 · Workflow change: what gets redesigned around the system

  • Before: once a month, Raghav pulls data from SAP, works through it in Excel and writes a forecast. Procurement then raises purchase orders in Zoho.
  • After: every Monday and Thursday the analyst uploads the SAP export. The engine applies Raghav's written rules to every SKU, and Gemini drafts a short planner brief.
  • Raghav handles only the exceptions flagged MANUAL_REVIEW, about 15–20%.
  • Procurement approves, edits or rejects each reorder in the app, then exports the approved orders, grouped by distributor, into Zoho.
  • What the system does not do: AI never sets a quantity, and no order is sent without a named approver. The thresholds are a configuration file that Raghav owns.

4 · Measurement plan: in the world, not in the logs

  • Weeks 1–4, shadow mode. The engine runs alongside Raghav. We measure how often its decisions match his on the SKUs the rules cover, and we record why each disagreement happened.
  • Quarter 1, live on the SKUs the rules cover. We compare against the baseline:
    • stockout incidents, from distributor short-supply complaints and zero-stock weeks in SAP
    • write-off value, from the finance ledger
    • the share of SKUs within their target range at each run
    • time from SAP export to approved PO
  • Override rate. Every edit or rejection is logged with the approver and a reason. A rising override rate means the rules are drifting away from how planners actually decide.

5 · Kill criterion: when to stop and redesign

  • Shadow mode: the engine agrees with Raghav on fewer than 80% of the decisions the rules cover, even after the rules are tuned. That means the written rules aren't how he actually plans.
  • Live: after one quarter, losses on covered SKUs are not at least 10% below the baseline, or the override rate is above 30%.
  • Operations: IT cannot deliver the SAP export twice a week for 4 weeks in a row. Without fresh data the faster cycle is pointless.
